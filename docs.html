The clarity score

Every launch gets a single clarity score from 0 to 100. Higher means the launch is easier to read: healthy liquidity, sane holder distribution, and coherent signals. Lower means it's noisy, uneven, or hard to verify.

The score is a weighted blend of five signals (below). It's deterministic — the same token always reads the same way, so you can compare launches fairly instead of chasing a number that jumps around.

70 – 100
✓ SAFE

50 – 69

0 – 49

Hard to read or high risk. Labeled NOISY · decision SKIP.

The 5 signals

The score is built from five weighted signals. Liquidity and holder shape carry the most weight because they're the hardest to fake.

SignalWeightWhat it reads
liquidity path25%Depth and health of the launch's liquidity — can you actually get in and out.
holder shape25%Holder count and top-holder concentration. Fewer whales holding a huge % = riskier.
social velocity20%Momentum and attention around the launch, relative to its size.
contract claims20%How well the contract's promises match its on-chain behavior.
narrative fit10%Whether the story is coherent and consistent, not a copy-paste of the last trend.

Safety verdicts

Every scan and every row in the public watchlist carries a safety badge, so you get the call at a glance without reading the number:

  • ✓ SAFE — score 70+. The launch reads clean. Still your call, but nothing obvious is off.
  • ⚠ CAUTION — score 50–69. Readable but uneven; the verdict names the specific weak signal to watch.
  • ✗ NOT SAFE — score under 50. Noisy or hard to verify. Handle with care.

The verdict line is written from the strongest and weakest signals, e.g. "Strong liquidity path; watch social velocity. Clean read."

FAQ

Is this financial advice?

No. Scores are informational signals about how readable a launch is — not a recommendation to buy or sell. Always do your own research.

Do I have to hold $KRILL to use it?

No. The score, the full 5-signal breakdown, the AI verdict, and the Ask KRILL agent are all free for everyone — no token, no wallet, no signature. Holding $KRILL only adds power-user extras like priority scans, custom watchlists, and launch alerts.

Do I need to connect a wallet?

Never. Tier checks are read-only balance lookups from a pasted address. KRILL never asks for a signature or transaction.

Why does the same token always get the same score?

The engine is deterministic by design. A score that flickers every refresh is noise; a stable score lets you compare launches fairly over time.

What chain is this?

KRILL runs via Virtuals on Robinhood Chain. On-chain reads (holders, supply, top-holder %) come straight from the chain.
